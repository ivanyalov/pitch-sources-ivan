# Review and improve a pitch

Use this on a completed workbook or an existing deck. Review the argument before polishing the slides. The questions are our practical adaptation of the sources linked below; they are not a validated scoring instrument.

## Review sequence

| Check | Question for the reviewer | Revision when the answer is unclear |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Offering | Can I describe what it does, for whom, and its stage? | Replace vague category language with the actual customer experience. |
| Need | What indicates that this problem matters to this segment? | Add relevant evidence, narrow the claim, or mark it as a hypothesis. |
| Connection | Why should this solution address that need? | Explain the mechanism and the research insight connecting them. |
| Opportunity | Can I follow the calculation and identify its inputs? | Define segment, units, geography, period, and assumptions. |
| Choice | Why would the customer change from their current approach? | Compare meaningful benefits and costs, including adoption effort. |
| Business | Who pays, through what channel, and with what main costs? | State the initial business hypothesis and its supporting evidence. |
| Progress | What has been learned or achieved, over what period? | Separate completed results from plans and identify what each result supports. |
| Execution | What is the next consequential uncertainty and how will it be tested? | Connect the next milestone, resources, and success criterion to it. |

For each weak point, decide whether it needs clearer wording, a better model, new evidence, or a narrower claim. Better wording alone cannot repair an unsupported business assumption.

## Clarity and narrative

- Ask an unfamiliar reader to explain the offering back without extra explanation. Compare their account with your intended meaning.
- Read the headlines in order. Check whether each adds a necessary step, and whether a later claim contradicts an earlier definition.
- Give repeated explanations one home. Keep the opening brief, the solution mechanism in section 3, and the comparison in section 5.
- Use a visual when the relationship is easier to see: a customer workflow, a labelled calculation, a fair comparison table, or a result against a baseline. A decorative infographic does not supply evidence.
- Check that the main point and necessary labels remain readable at presentation size. Move supporting detail to the appendix and rehearse within the time available.

Sources: [YC on explaining the company](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-pitch-your-company/), [YC on slide clarity](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-design-a-better-pitch-deck/), and [IDEO on adapting a pitch to an audience](https://www.designkit.org/methods/create-a-pitch.html). The short YC Demo Day context does not set the slide count for this course pitch.

## Evidence and assumption checks

- Keep the source and its limits beside the claim: who or what was studied, when, and in what setting.
- Distinguish reported interest, observed behavior, payment, repeated use, and technical performance. Each answers a different question.
- Use the same user, buyer, segment, geography, and timeframe consistently across sections, or explain a deliberate change.
- Identify a future benefit as a target until a suitable test supports it. Distinguish confirmed partnerships from intended ones.
- Check that a forecast is labelled as a model and that a working prototype is not being presented as proof of demand.

Sources: [Strategyzer on evidence strength](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/how-strong-is-your-innovation-evidence), [a16z on early-market interpretation and alternatives](https://a16z.com/need-more-time-or-lack-of-product-market-fit-guideposts-for-tech-founders-going-to-market-when-no-market-exists/), and [Heilmeier on risks and tests](https://www.darpa.mil/about/heilmeier-catechism).

## Examples of structural repairs

These are invented wording patterns for practice, not customer findings or startup case studies. Brackets require your own evidence.

| Weak draft | Better structure |
| --- | --- |
| "An innovative platform for everyone" | "[Offering] helps [specific customer] perform [task]. [Stage]." |
| "People love our prototype" | "In [test/context], [participants] did or reported [specific finding]. This supports [limited conclusion]; [remaining uncertainty] is untested." |
| "We only need 1% of a huge market" | "For [segment/geography/period], [customer count] x [purchase behavior] x [price] gives [defined estimate]. [Inputs] remain assumptions." |
| "We have no competitors" | "Customers currently use [alternative]. We compare [benefit and cost criteria], supported by [evidence]." |
| "Next: launch and scale" | "Next we will test [assumption] with [method], using [resources] by [time]. Success means [observable criterion]." |
