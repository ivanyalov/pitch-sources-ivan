# Build or improve a pitch

Use this guide to turn an idea into a supported argument. You can read everything on GitHub and copy the workbook into Google Docs, Word, or your own Markdown file. No software installation is needed to use the materials.

The default is our [eight-section outline](pitch-structure.md): an early-stage startup pitch of about 15 minutes, a separate hardware demonstration, and no funding ask. Keep that structure while drafting; adapt the amount of detail to your audience and evidence.

## 1. Establish the idea and the audience

Copy the [pitch workbook](../templates/pitch-workbook.md). Fill in its brief: audience, what they need to understand, stage, time available, and demonstration arrangements.

If the idea is still unclear, first answer four NABC questions in rough notes:

- Whose important need are you addressing, and what indicates that it matters?
- What approach could address it?
- What benefits could justify the customer's money, effort, and other costs?
- What alternatives would the customer compare it with?

Unanswered questions identify research to do. They do not require invented answers. NABC prepares the value proposition; it does not replace the full startup outline. [Framework reference](https://www.wpi.edu/about/value-creation-initiative) · [NABC slides](../library/pdfs/nabc-value-proposition.pdf)

## 2. Draft the argument before designing slides

Fill the workbook's eight sections with short notes. For each main claim, record the supporting source or observation and what remains assumed. Use the [full outline](pitch-structure.md) for coverage; leave unknowns visible.

The methods have different jobs in this process:

| Section | Method to use | What it helps you examine |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Offering | [YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-pitch-your-company/) | Whether a newcomer can understand what you do |
| 2. Problem | [IDEO](https://www.designkit.org/methods/frame-your-design-challenge.html) and NABC Need | Whether the problem is meaningful and open to different solutions |
| 3. Solution | NABC Approach and Benefits/costs | How the approach produces value and what it demands of customers |
| 4. Opportunity | YC and [Sequoia](https://sequoiacap.com/article/writing-a-business-plan) | Segment, commercial scale, timing, and explicit model assumptions |
| 5. Choice | NABC Competition and [a16z](https://a16z.com/need-more-time-or-lack-of-product-market-fit-guideposts-for-tech-founders-going-to-market-when-no-market-exists/) | Relevant alternatives, including the current workaround |
| 6. Business | YC and Sequoia | Payer, revenue, acquisition, and the main costs |
| 7. Progress | YC and [Strategyzer](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/how-strong-is-your-innovation-evidence) | What completed work establishes and the limits of the evidence |
| 8. Execution | Sequoia and [Heilmeier](https://www.darpa.mil/about/heilmeier-catechism) | Capability, uncertainty, resources, and measurable next tests |

This mapping is our editorial synthesis, not a jointly endorsed framework. Read the specific source when a section needs help; reading the whole library is optional.

## 3. Connect the sections

Read only your proposed section headlines. Do they explain the offering, need, approach, opportunity, customer choice, business, progress, and next step? Then check the dependencies:

- The solution addresses the problem you actually established.
- The market estimate counts the same customer segment and payer described elsewhere.
- The comparison uses the benefits and costs those customers care about.
- Progress supports claims already made; the next tests address what is still uncertain.

If two sections make the same point, give the explanation one home and use a short reference elsewhere. If a link depends on an assumption, identify it explicitly.

## 4. Study an example for the particular gap

Open the [guided examples](examples.md). Use Intercom for a short problem/solution sequence, LinkedIn with its founder commentary for argument and critique, and YC's illustrated article for slide clarity. Each entry explains the useful feature and the limitations of borrowing it.

Copy the reasoning technique you need. Keep your own evidence, audience, and stage visible.

## 5. Review, revise, and rehearse

Use the [review checklist](improve-your-pitch.md) on the draft. Ask a reader unfamiliar with the idea to explain it back without your help. Record where their interpretation differs from yours, revise the underlying explanation, and then design or edit the slides.

Rehearse with the actual time limit. Keep the central argument in the presentation and move supporting material to the appendix. Treat the hardware demonstration as a separate test of selected capabilities; a working prototype does not by itself establish customer demand.

The workbook and review checklist are preparation tools. The concise [pitch structure](pitch-structure.md) remains the shared section specification.
