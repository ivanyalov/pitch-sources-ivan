# Working principles

These are our editorial choices for an early-stage pitch. Source advice has different audiences and stages; the principles below do not claim universal agreement.

1. **Explain the offering early.** A listener should understand what the product does and whom it serves before the detailed argument. [YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-pitch-your-company/)
2. **Show why the need matters.** Use specific behavior, past events, and credible evidence. Identify the context and limits of each finding. [IDEO.org](https://www.designkit.org/methods/frame-your-design-challenge.html)
3. **Separate the user from the buyer when their decisions differ.** Describe the value and effort for each relevant role. [IDEO.org](https://www.designkit.org/methods/define-your-audience.html)
4. **Connect evidence to decisions.** Explain the insight and why it led to the proposed approach. Mark alternative interpretations and untested assumptions. [YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-pitch-your-company/)
5. **Match the claim to its evidence.** Stated interest, observed use, payment, retention, and technical performance answer different questions. [Strategyzer](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/how-strong-is-your-innovation-evidence)
6. **Compare the alternatives customers actually consider.** Include current workarounds and doing nothing. Do not choose comparison axes merely to make the product win. [a16z](https://a16z.com/need-more-time-or-lack-of-product-market-fit-guideposts-for-tech-founders-going-to-market-when-no-market-exists/)
7. **Evaluate benefits relative to customer costs.** Consider money, effort, setup, maintenance, and other relevant sacrifices. [NABC](https://www.wpi.edu/about/value-creation-initiative)
8. **Make slides easy to understand.** Favor one main point, legible text, meaningful graphics, direct labels, and explicit takeaways. [YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-design-a-better-pitch-deck/) and [Assertion-Evidence](https://writing.engr.psu.edu/assertion_evidence_EA.html)
9. **Give models visible boundaries.** State the segment, units, period, sources, and assumptions. Optional models earn their place by clarifying a decision. [Sequoia](https://sequoiacap.com/article/writing-a-business-plan)
10. **Show progress and the next uncertainty.** Summarize what changed, the important risks, and the next measurable test. [Heilmeier](https://www.darpa.mil/about/heilmeier-catechism)
11. **Support the narrative with contrast.** Show what becomes different for the customer while keeping intended outcomes distinct from demonstrated results. [Duarte](https://www.duarte.com/blog/ultimate-guide-to-contrast/)
12. **Adapt to the actual audience and slot.** The short investor-deck prescriptions in the source set are context-dependent. Use the eight sections as preparation responsibilities and rehearse the resulting narrative for the time available.

## Section boundaries

| Sections | Boundary |
| --- | --- |
| 1 and 3 | Identify the offering briefly in 1; explain the experience, mechanism, and value in 3. |
| 2 and 4 | Describe whose problem matters in 2; estimate commercial opportunity and timing in 4. |
| 3 and 5 | Explain the solution in 3; compare it with alternatives in 5. |
| 7 and 8 | Report completed work and learning in 7; explain capability and future execution in 8. |

Keep evidence beside the claim it supports. Section 7 is a synthesis of progress, not a repository for every research result.
