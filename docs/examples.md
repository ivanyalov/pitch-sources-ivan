# Guided examples and structure references

Use an example to answer a specific drafting question. A company's later success does not establish that every slide was effective, and an investor deck has a different purpose from a course pitch with no ask. The observations and exercises below are our editorial analysis.

Page numbers refer to positions in the included PDF, counting the cover as page 1. Original files remain unchanged. File provenance and extent are recorded in the [library](../library/README.md).

## Intercom: a short early-stage deck

[Open the eight-page PDF](../library/pdfs/intercom-first-pitch-deck.pdf) · [Founder context](https://www.intercom.com/blog/first-pitch-deck)

Useful for studying a compact progression from problem to product and progress. The founder identifies it as a late-2011 deck. This is an instructive example with visible weaknesses, not a model to copy wholesale.

| PDF page | What to inspect | Application to your draft |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3: Problem | A defined customer type, difficult tasks, and limitations of existing tools | Make the problem specific; supply your own evidence for its importance. |
| 4: Solution | Product behavior described through concrete functions | Explain what customers can actually do. Select what matters for the need. |
| 5: Market | A broad category figure and future expansion | Challenge whether the category figure measures the addressable opportunity; build a bounded bottom-up estimate. |
| 6: Alternatives | Categories of existing tools | Identify what customers currently combine or substitute; add fair decision criteria. |
| 7: Progress | Development status and qualitative beta enthusiasm | Separate shipped work from reported interest; define the tests behind validation claims. |

Page 8 is a funding request. It belongs to the original context and is not required in our default course outline. The early team placement and typography are also choices to examine rather than prescriptions.

**Exercise:** compare sections 2, 3, and 7 of your draft with these pages. Identify one explanation to simplify and one claim that needs stronger support.

## LinkedIn: argument with the founder's own critique

[Open the 37-page PDF](../library/pdfs/linkedin-series-b-2004.pdf) · [Read Reid Hoffman's annotations](https://www.reidhoffman.org/linkedin-pitch-to-greylock/)

Useful for studying how a pitch addresses objections. This is a 2004 Series B investor deck, so its stage and length differ from our format. Read the founder commentary alongside it: Hoffman explicitly identifies both stylistic and substantive errors.

- PDF page 2 shows several proposed revenue streams. Read the commentary on the risk of presenting multiple business models; a historical outcome does not make that a default recommendation.
- PDF pages 3-4 contrast existing directory-based search with the proposed network approach. Examine whether the distinction clarifies a mechanism and what evidence would be needed to support the claimed benefit.
- Select one explanation from the founder commentary that changes your interpretation of a slide. Apply the underlying question to your draft rather than copying the slide.

The mirror PDF contains the deck; it does not contain the full commentary. Do not use its 37-page length as a target for a 15-minute presentation.

## Slide clarity: before-and-after examples

[YC: How to Design a Better Pitch Deck](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-design-a-better-pitch-deck/)

This illustrated article is particularly useful for comparing an early explanation with a clearer revision. Study the Shred Video and Afrostream examples: what becomes easier to understand when the description and visual hierarchy change? Apply the same question to one slide of your own.

It is a visual essay about a much shorter Demo Day pitch, not a downloadable startup deck or a slide-count prescription for this project.

For another visual method, use the [Assertion-Evidence checklist PDF](../library/pdfs/assertion-evidence-checklist.pdf). The [editable example](../library/presentations/assertion-evidence-movie-magic.ppt) and [template](../library/presentations/assertion-evidence-template.ppt) are legacy PowerPoint files for technical presentations, not startup pitch examples.

## Live delivery and critique

- [a16z Pitch Room: Sandbox VR](https://a16z.com/the-a16z-pitch-room-sandbox-vr/): the publisher presents a pitch followed by investor discussion. Use it to compare the intended message with the audience's questions. It is a streaming reference; no local video or standalone slide file is included.
- [Nancy Duarte: The Secret Structure of Great Talks](https://www.ted.com/talks/nancy_duarte_the_secret_structure_of_great_talks): use the product-launch analysis to examine contrast and pacing. A launch keynote and an early-stage startup pitch have different evidence needs.

These video suggestions are based on their publishers' descriptions; they are not a claim that the full recordings have been reviewed here. Record one useful technique, its limits, and the change you will test in your own presentation.
